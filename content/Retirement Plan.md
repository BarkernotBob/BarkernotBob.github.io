# Overview
## A purpose for each account
1. **Taxable Brokerage:** Taking into account long term capital gains, this account can function similar to a Roth IRA, except that the LT gains are still taxed at the 4.74% local rate. The benefits are that even the gains are accessible and [[Tax Loss Harvesting|tax loss harvesting]] is possible. The drawback is really just having to keep in mind timing before making any sales to ensure that no short term capital gains are incurred, and the additional local tax. **PURPOSE:** The most versatile account, usable before retirement and giving the opportunity for tax loss harvesting which no other account gives. Can be sold at LTCG, then converted to a tax deferred account and at that point will function the same as if it had been in the tax deferred account the whole time. 
2. **Roth IRA:** Same as above, with no chance of incurring taxes once money is deposited. Account will be used during early retirement where principal can be withdrawn fee free. **Purpose:** Principle usable before retirement, and 401k funds will be sent here in early retirement / low income years to attempt to avoid all income taxes on these funds by utilizing a [[Roth Conversion Ladder]]. 
3. **401k / Traditional IRA:** This account has the benefit of likely being the largest due to employer matching. Whenever I finish my job, I will transfer these funds to a traditional IRA and begin transferring an amount yearly into the Roth IRA (Roth Conversion Ladder). Since I won't be working or will have a low paying ministry job, income will be low. This means that I can transfer funds over up to the standard deduction and pay no Federal taxes on them (local taxes will apply no matter what). **Purpose:** Seeking to avoid taxes on the front and back end. Emergency protection as I can take a loan out and pay myself back the principal and interest. 
4. **HSA:** Most valuable money possible. Whether used for medical or used for non-med in retirement, you avoid the 7.65% social/medicaid tax. Then, if used for medical, you avoid the 12% Federal and 4.74% taxes. When compared to regular income, that's a 24.4% tax savings! **Purpose:** Grants the largest tax savings possible. Covers medical expenses now and therefore functions as a bit of an emergency fund. Flexible at retirement as it can be used as an IRA with no RMD's if necessary. 
5. **403b(9):** This is a ministry plan that has an added benefit for housing expenses. Will plan to put in the full housing expenses amount.

# Saving Money when income is low
## Lowering Taxes
### [EITC](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/taxes/can-you-take-earned-income-tax-credit#earned-income-tax-credit-2025)
* up to $7,152. Must have some earned income that year and dependents. Refundable. Income qualified. Less than $11,000 in investment income. 
* Basically (as of 2026) this means keeping your income right around **$30,000** for a maximum benefit of ~ **$8000**. This income is calculated **Before** the standard deduction. This income includes regular income, as well as Roth conversions from an IRA over to a Roth. Your regular income needs to be at least ~ **$17,000**, and the rest can be regular or conversion income. 
* [Calculator](https://apps.irs.gov/app/eitc/results/)
*
### [Saver's Credit](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-savings-contributions-credit-savers-credit)
* Up to $2000 if you contribute at least $4000 to a retirement account. Traditional or Roth, but **Rollovers do not count**. *Income qualified.*
