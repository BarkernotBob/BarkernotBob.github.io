This is simply the fact that 2 variables which correlate (coincide, one thing seeming to rise or fall with the other) are not necessarily related in any specific way based simply on the correlation.

To give some credit, it is certainly true in practical life that unproven correlation is often strong evidence of causation. The most important thing is to ensure that:
1. We are wary of our self bias that will see preferred correlation as proof and disliked correlation as irrelevant. 
2. We are wary of marketing or any kind of teaching which will try to lie with spurious correlation.
3. We understand the strength of a correlation, consider alternative possibilities, and only build further ideas or action on a correlation when that action is warranted.

A few ways this error can manifest
1. B caused A, A didn't cause B
2. A third factor is actually what caused A and B | See [[Confounding]]
3. The relationship is bidirectional, where both are interrelated, causing and caused in some measure by one another
4. Coincidence rather than any real link is the reason for the correlation

## Causal analysis (WIKI)

[Causal analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causal_analysis "Causal analysis") is the field of [experimental design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_of_experiments "Design of experiments") and [statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics "Statistics") pertaining to establishing cause and effect.[4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation_does_not_imply_causation#cite_note-4) Typically it involves establishing four elements: [correlation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation "Correlation"), sequence in time (that is, causes must occur before their proposed effect), a plausible physical or [information-theoretical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_theory "Information theory") mechanism for an observed effect to follow from a possible cause, and eliminating the possibility of [common and alternative ("special") causes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_cause_and_special_cause_\(statistics\) "Common cause and special cause (statistics)"). Such analysis usually involves one or more [controlled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experiment#Controlled_experiment "Experiment") or [natural experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_experiment "Natural experiment").[5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation_does_not_imply_causation#cite_note-5)
