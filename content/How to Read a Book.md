# Preface
* What everyone needs in reading is the ability to read different things at different and appropriate speeds. The aim is to "read better, always better, but sometimes slower, sometimes faster."
* Reading instruction falls off ~ 6th grade, although there is still much to be learned. High schoolers, on average, have been shown to be inadequate in indicating central thoughts, points of emphasis and subordination, or in critically considering an argument. 
    * *My thought here* is that, much like driving, reading is a skill that most are likely to consider achieved once they have basic competency. Any attempt to challenge areas of growth are likely to be received with defensiveness. 
    * This reminds me of a point in the book, The Hermeneutical Spiral: *"When teaching Bible study method seminars to church groups, I have discovered that **the most difficult thing for the novice to learn is how to skim each paragraph and summarize its main point.** People get bogged down in details and never seem to surface for air."* [^1]
* A reader should be able to read multiple sources on a topic and clearly grasp the complementary and conflicting things they teach.
# Part 1 - The Dimensions of Reading
## Chapter 1 - The Activity and Art of Reading
* A reader's **main purpose** in reading books is to gain **increased understanding**. 
* Modernity, and especially our inventions, have greatly increased the **ease** at which we may attain **knowledge** through things like the radio, tv, internet, social media, online news, email lists, and the like. But "We do not have to know everything about something in order to understand it; Too many facts are often as much of an obstacle to understanding as too few. There is a sense in which we moderns are inundated with facts to the detriment of understanding."*p.4* 
    * *This reminds me* of [2 Tim 3:7](https://ref.ly/2Tim3.7) and [Acts 17:21](https://ref.ly/ac17.21). First, Paul says that in the last days one way that the sin of man will be manifested is in people always learning but never able to arrive at the knowledge of the truth. In Acts, we hear that the pagans in Athens spent all their time in telling or hearing something new. This was a mark of the city, as we're told that this was what the foreigners did as well. For the christian, while we aim to learn and grow, we know that knowledge alone simply puffs up. We must learn to understand, and focus our understanding on God's revelation through Christ, with our intention being an outflow of love for others. 
* Our media is often designed to make thinking **seem** unnecessary. Because the information is packaged so that the receiver can ingest the information with minimal effort, thinking can easily be sidestepped without even recognizing that this has happened.

## Chapter 2 - The Levels of Reading
* The levels of reading are:
    1. **Elementary Reading / Initial Reading**
        1. The question here is simply "what does the sentence say". This is not in any deeper sense than having basic literacy able to comprehend the individual symbols and how they string together phonetically.
        2. Even well seasoned readers can run into roadblocks here. Consider:
            1. Other languages. If a work has not been translated, how are we to understand it?
                1. **When we consider scripture**, it seems sometimes elementary understanding is skipped over in our debates. 2 sides will argue over an issue as if it was a difference in interpretation or hermeneutics. But if there is a disagreement over the meaning of words, this is an issue of elementary reading. Since this is the first level of reading, if it is skipped, we cannot ever hope to be sure that what we learn in levels 2-4 is solid. 
            2. Development of language. Old English may be 95% understandable with our current vocabulary, but whenever we run into the 5% that isn't we'll have to do the work of learning the elementary meaning of the words before we can start considering anything further.
            3. Even in modern english, we can still run into trouble with elementary reading. I sometimes still struggle to understand how a semicolon is used. There are words I do not know, and some words I do know which have more technical focus that I readily understand.
    2. **Inspectional Reading / Systematic Skimming**
        1. The goal here: **How to get the most out of a written work in the shortest amount of time.** If given 15-30 minutes, how would you attack a written work that would take you 10 hours to read straight through? Definitionally, Inspectional reading cannot get everything possible out of a book. Your goal is to find out everything the surface of the book can teach you, and you assume it can teach you a lot. Questions asked here:
            1. What is the book about? 
            2. What is its structure? 
            3. What are its parts? 
        2. Inspectional reading is ;; The art of *Skimming Systematically*
<!--SR:!2026-01-18,4,270-->
        3. **Most readers skip inspectional reading completely**. The result: "They are thus faced with the task of achieving a superficial knowledge of the book at the same time that they are trying to understand it. That compounds the difficulty"
    3. **Analytical reading / Thorough Reading**
        1. Analytical reading is the opposite side of the coin of inspectional reading. While inspectional reading was asking the question, "how do I get the most complete understanding of the book given a limited amount of time?", Analytical reading asks "How do I get the best and most complete understanding of a book given unlimited time?" This reading is always very active, and requires an organized and systematic approach to reading if you want to achieve great success.
        2. **Francis Bacon said**, "some books are to be tasted, others to be swallowed, and some few to be chewed and digested." Chewing and digesting is the work of Analytical Reading.
        3. This level of reading is usually not necessary at all for reading that is only for entertainment or which is just information gathering. It is however indispensable if your reading is for the sake of understanding. Without skill in analytical reading, going from a state of less understanding to more understanding is nearly impossible.
    4. **Syntopical Reading / Comparative reading**
        1. At this stage, the reader is taking and synthesizing all things that have been read from various sources on the same topic. At this stage, the reader can begin going beyond what any of the works individually have covered and construct their own analysis of the topic. Essentially, when this stage is performed correctly, the whole is greater than the sum of its parts.

# Part 2 - The Third Level of Reading: Analytical Reading
## Chapter 6-7, Pigeonholing and X-raying a book
### The first stage of analytical reading: 4 rules for finding out what a book's about
* Here, we are trying to answer the first question of Analytical Reading: "**What is the book about as a whole**"
    * How often do we fail to start with this question when reading the bible?

#### Rule 1: Classify the book according to kind and subject matter

#### Rule 2: State what the whole book is about with the utmost brevity
* Every book worth reading has a unity and an organization to of parts
* The more briefly you can express the point of a book, the better you understand it
* Every good story has a main thread of plot that ties everything together
#### Rule 3: Outline its major parts in their order and relation
* While expressing the point of the work as one thing is important, this step shows how that one united work is made up of a complex unity. An organized many. A set of organically related things.
* A house is one thing, but it is made up of a great many parts. It is *true* to call it one thing, but you do not fully understand it without understanding how all the parts and their purposes make up the whole.
* The best books are those that have the most intelligent structure.

> *"You cannot comprehend a whole without somehow seeing its parts. But it is also true that unless you grasp the organization of its parts, you cannot know the whole comprehensively."*

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">The reason a good sermon should have structure is that the bible has structure. A running commentary on a set of verses that sees them as disconnected from each other, the book, and the arc of scripture is not truly expositing the text. </mark> 

#### Rule 4: Define the problems the author is trying to solve
* Essentially, why did this book need to be written? What question did the author set out to answer? Does the book provide a comprehensive and satisfactory answer? 
## Chapter 8 - Coming to Terms with an Author
### The second stage: 4 rules for finding what a book says
#### Rule 1: Come to terms by interpreting key words
This rule has 2 parts: 
1. Locate the important words, the ones that make a difference.
    1. For you, any word that you have trouble with is important. If you stumble or can't tell what sense it's being used in, this word (term) should be studied further.
    2. For the author, any word specially called out through typography, explained in detail, or clearly being used in a special sense is also a term to study further.
2. Precisely determine the meaning of those words as used.
    1. You should seek to determine the meaning through the context. Although this word may not be clear to you, most of the words around it should be. If you can understand those and work out how they're related, you should be closer to understanding the term.
    2. Author's can use a **word** in more than one **sense** each comprising a different **term**. When this happens, you should seek to understand the meaning of each and then see **how these terms are related**.
    3. Author's can also use many words for the same term (often simply because repetitive language is boring), so terms should not be rigidly tied to a singular word unless there's good reason to do so. 
* Nearly every word is ambiguous in that it can have many meanings. The goal here is to understand the sense in which the author is speaking for their key terms.
* *Expository* works seek to **Remove** ambiguity, but *Poetic* works are **built on** it. 
    * In scripture, are there times where these will both be at play? Is it a problem or a blessed truth that wind and Spirit are the same word in both Hebrew and Greek? It may make the work of interpretation harder, but I believe this is actually a gift from God to truly convey through language his glorious words.
