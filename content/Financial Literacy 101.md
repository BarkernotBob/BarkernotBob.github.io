[[Financial Literacy 102]]

*Went back to my Econ notes from 2018* 

The reason we have to consider our finances is because every person is impacted by **Scarcity**. That is to say, both our resources and our time are limited. This scarcity inevitably leads to having to make choices. 
Every time we choose to spend our time on something we are also choosing *not* to spend that same time on all the other things that we might wish we were doing. 
In the same way, every time you choose to buy a coffee, you are also choosing *not* to use that money towards all the other desirable options you could consider.
This is an example of **Opportunity Cost**, the fact that every choice you make in life, whether you acknowledge it or not, is also you missing out on the next best thing you could have chosen to do.

Since resources are scarce and every decision comes with a cost, we are forced to prioritize based on what we **value**. All actions should be prioritized so that what you value most is acquired and achieved, and your opportunity costs are only the things you value less. 


## So what is <mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">value</mark>?

1. So what is value?
    1. *the regard that something is held to deserve; the importance, worth, or usefulness of something*
    2. As we consider money's value, the first thing we have to see is that it's value is *extrinsic,* it means nothing by itself. It only has value because people have collectively agreed to use it as a medium of exchange. If people stopped accepting money in exchange for goods and services, you'd be forced to find an alternative use for it, and you'll find out very quickly that it's pretty bad at doing anything else useful. So money's value is not intrinsic, but it can be exchanged for things which do have intrinsic value. This is sometimes called value in use;" the other, "value in exchange."
    3. But even value is often both *subjective* and *situational*. 
        1. Think first of the subjectivity. You may think that a meal from Raising Cane's tastes great and is worth $12, but I certainly don't. The difference between us is not the amount of $ we pay for the meal, but how highly we each value the alternative meals we could get with the same amount of $. 
        2. Next, the situational value. While Tarek's AI glasses certainly cost more $ than a cold bottled water if you head over to BestBuy, which of the two would you choose if your car broke down in the middle of nowhere, 100 degrees outside, no cell service, no one in sight, closest city 10 miles away?
<mark style="background: #FFB86CA6;">So to sum up, we should all seek to prioritize our lives such that the choices we make align with and bring us closer to the things we value. </mark>

Now before we move on, I would be remiss if I didn't mention that the bible has much to say on this point. One specific point worth considering is Paul's words to the pastor Timothy. He said, *"But those who desire to be rich fall into temptation, into a snare, into many senseless and harmful desires that plunge people into ruin and destruction. 10 For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evils. Specifically, note that Paul assumes people will have desires, cravings, and loves which are out of alignment, sinful, and harmful to themselves. So the biblical exhortation here, that I pass on to you, is to know that your own value system in its natural state is out of alignment, and just one way that can express itself is in a "love of money" and "desire to be rich."

# Why <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">yesterday’s dollar was better than today's</mark>
1. The <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">time value of money</mark> and the law of time preference. 
    1. First, I need to introduce you to a great latin phrase used in economics, "ceteris paribus." This means, "All other things being equal." Essentially, ceteris paribus allows us to make a comparison between 2 options and say one variable has changed but all other variables between the 2 options are the same.
    2. With this in mind, the economic law of time preference is that ceteris paribus, we prefer present satisfaction to future satisfaction. So if Brotherhood said they were now offering the choice to all employees to continue on the normal pay cycle or get paid a lump sum today for all the money you would've accrued bi-weekly over the rest of the year, which do you think would be chosen by more employees? It's a no brainer. Getting the money now reduces my risk of not being able to pay for an emergency that might arise, gives me more time to invest if I choose to, and just gives me more purchasing power day to day for the things I need. This will come back up when we talk about interest, but for now we just need to establish a simple fact, 
    > *The earlier in your life you acquire a dollar, ceteris paribus, the more value you will derive from that dollar.*
2. The second reason yesterday's dollar was better than today's is because of Inflation. Inflation is essentially the fact that overtime a currency devalues. Said another way, generally prices go up over time. On average in the US inflation has been pacing about 2-3% per year. This doesn't sound like a whole lot, but this adds up very quickly over time.
    1. How long would it take for money's value to be halved at a flat 3% inflation rate? *less than 25 years*
    2. **Show chart**
So to summarize, money is worth more the earlier you have it, both because it affords you more options and opportunities, and because it will be devalued as time goes on.
