## Example (Losses)
1. Buy $5000 of Total Market. `[!!info|This is because the S&P 500 is what I have in my Roth/401K|var(--color-blue-rgb)]` 
2. Value decreases to $4000
3. Sell at a $1000 loss
4. Buy $4000 of S&P 500 in Roth account
5. Taxes are marked with a $1000 loss, leading to a $120 Federal and $47 tax decrease. 
6. New growth will be tax free, but of course the money put into the Roth can be withdrawn tax free at any time. 

## Example 2 (Gains after a year)
Buy $5000 of Total Market. `[!!info|This is because the S&P 500 is what I have in my Roth/401K|var(--color-blue-rgb)]` 
2. Value increases to $6000
3. Wait 1 year before Selling at a $1000 gain
4. Sale is long term capital gains and therefore not taxed at all

## Example 3 (Gains after a year)
Buy $5000 of Total Market. `[!!info|This is because the S&P 500 is what I have in my Roth/401K|var(--color-blue-rgb)]` 
2. Value increases to $6000
3. Sell immediately before a year
4. Sale is short term capital gains and counted as extra income of $120 and $47. 
5. Paid taxes, but growth well outpaced the safer asset class.
