**Logos Bible FREE Version: Powerful, Comprehensive, Simple**

Logos bible is an incredible tool for studying the scriptures. But for the average christian looking for ways to improve their bible study, Logos bible is usually seen as too expensive, too complicated, or simply as a tool that adds no practical value. 

  

Well in the next 10 minutes I want to convince you that Logos bible can actually be very simple, is a practical aid to the average christian’s bible study, and that it can do all of this for less than a penny…that means for free. While this video will only show you _what_ logos can do, if you’d like to set this up for yourself keep an eye out for my next video where I will show you the simple setup steps.

  

**Reading, highlighting, note taking**

We’ll start by looking at [Deuteronomy 32](https://ref.ly/logosref/bible$2Besv.5.32). As I’m reading, I can apply my custom highlighter styles easily by selecting text and clicking a single key on the keyboard. And since I’d like to start a note on the passage to document my observations, I’ll click the letter n on the keyboard and a new note will be opened which is connected to the text I selected.

  

**Inline Search, Passage Lists, Fuzzy Search**

Now, as I’m reading, this language of the rock interests me. I can see from the context that this is referencing God, and if I right click on the word I can see that Logos has also identified this as a reference to God. I’d like to see other places where this language for God is used, all I have to do is double click the word to bring it up in an inline search, then I further narrow my search to only include results where this word is used in reference to God, and I can very quickly see that there are 18 occurrences of this Hebrew word used in reference to God.

I’d like to return to this list of passages later for further study, so I’ll save this as a passage list, and I’ll add a link to this passage list in my notes so that it’s easy to find later. 

Now I also remember as I’m reading that there’s a place in the New Testament where Jesus is equated with a rock, but I can’t quite remember where this is or how it’s worded. So I do my best and search what I remember(Jesus was the Rock), but I’m obviously not quite right because I’m getting no results. But if I head over to fuzzy search, Logos now tries to understand what I mean even if I didn’t get the words right, and in this case it did a great job because the first result of [1 Cor 10:4](https://ref.ly/logosref/bible$2Besv.67.10.4) is exactly what I was looking for.

I’d like to follow up on this passage later as well, so I’ll jot it down in my passage note. As always, scripture references are transformed into links to the passage for easy reference later.

  

**Translation Info Pane, BWS**

Now I’m curious to learn a bit more about this word Justice. I double click on the word to perform another inline search, and here I see that the same Hebrew word is translated differently in verse 4 than it is in verse 41. To get a quick idea of how my other English bibles handle the word in these verses, I can click the word and reference my info pane. Here I can see that verse 4 is variously translated, but verse 41 is unanimously “judgement.”

I’m curios what kind of range of meaning this word might have, so I head over to the Bible word study tool and notice that this word is translated in various different ways depending on word form and context. (Turn off Inline Search)

While I’m looking at details of the Hebrew text, another tool I can use is a bible with color coordinated highlighting to help me quickly see more info about the original language, like whether words are nouns or verbs, indicative or imperative, whether “you” is singular or plural, and when a word is only used one time in the whole bible. 

  

  

**Intertext, Variants, text comparison tool, NET bible**

Moving forward, one thing I always want to consider in my study are examples of intertext, which is when the Old Testament is referenced by the New, and this purple slash is letting me know that verse 8 has an intertext connection. Since this verse is active, my passage guide shows me that this is alluded to in Acts 17:26, which I can hover over to read the passage and better understand how this Old Testament passage was used in the New.

Another important detail I like to keep an eye out for are textual variants which could lead to differences in translations, and this red slash is letting me know that one of these variants exists for verse 8.

To pursue this further, I can open up the text comparison tool to see if there’s differences among my English translations. I notice that the end of this verse has two very different renderings, with some saying “sons of God” and others “children of Israel”. 

I wonder what might be leading to this difference, so I open up the NET bible website from my quicklink panel, navigate to Deuteronomy 32 verse 8, and check if there’s a translation note. I can see that there is in fact a translation note here, so I’ll save this web page and add it to my notes to read up on further.

I’d also like to see what my commentaries say on this passage, and since these commentaries are in the same link set as my bible, they’re already waiting for me on Deuteronomy 32:8.

In addition, if I’d like to learn more about this phrase “Sons of God”, I can plug this into the Factbook and bring together dictionary entries, biblical passages, original language study, and more. 

  

**Studying the broader section**

Now that I’ve looked at some of the details in the passage, I’d like to look more broadly at the whole chapter. To begin, I’ll open up the explorer tool and enter all the verses of Dt 32, and since I don’t remember how many verses there are I’ll just put in a wildly large number as the last verse and logos will fix it for me.

From here, I can see the events, people, places, and more that are referenced in the chapter. If I’d like to look into any of these, I can click the word and the factbook entry will open allowing me to research further. 

With a location, I can also see where this comes up in the Atlas tool, and from here I can see exactly where this is geographically, find out the distance between 2 locations, and even search for other locations on the map (Peor) to see where they are it in relation to this one. 

Similarly, I could open the reference to a person and the Factbook will bring together a wealth of information for further study.
