# Pre-Reading
1. Read the title and preface to **Pigeonhole** the book. Fill out the **Classification** property
2. Read the table of contents to gain a basic idea of the **Structure** of the book
3. Review the Index to identify key terms used
4. Identify what you think are key chapters and read the summary statements
5. **Skim** through looking for key arguments that capture the books **main contention**
6. Read the **last few pages**, expecting to find **summary statements** of the book

---
**From here, decide if the book is worth reading further**

| NO                                                                                                         | YES                                | LATER                                                                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Put the book away, with no plans to continue later. If you did glean anything from the book, note it down. | Move on to **Superficial Reading** | Make sure to note anything you learned about **Class, structure, and focus of the book**. This will let you pick the book back up more quickly in the future. |
# Superficial Reading
Here, you should read **quickly**. The goal is still to gain as much from the book in as little time as possible. You are giving the book a first pass, not expecting to glean everything it has to offer. This first step of reading will prepare you for deeper study later, should the book be worth further study. Because this is a quick reading focused more on the book as a whole, **don't get stuck in the details, lookup terms you don't understand, or reread sections you didn't immediately grasp.** The questions you should be answering and taking notes on here are:
1. What is the book about as a whole?
2. What are its main ideas & arguments?
3. What is the structure of the argument?
# Analytical Reading
1. **What is the book about as a whole:
    1. Classify the book according to kind and subject matter
    2. State what the whole book is about with the utmost brevity
    3. Outline its major parts in their order and relation
    4. Define the problems the author is trying to solve
2. **What does the book say**:
    1. Interpret key words
    2. Grasp leading propositions by dealing with the most important sentences
    3. Know the author's arguments
    4. Determine which of the author's problems are solved, which are not, and which he knows he didn't solve.
