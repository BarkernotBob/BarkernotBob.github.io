The Boots Theory

jun 21 2026·4 min read·updated jun 28
#finance#logic

Terry Pratchett put a whole theory of economic unfairness into a pair of boots. A good pair costs fifty dollars and lasts ten years. The cheap pair costs ten dollars and is destroyed in a season — so the person who can only ever afford the cheap pair pays more, over a decade, and still has wet feet.

"The reason that the rich were so rich was because they managed to spend less money."

The compounding cost of cheap

The boot is a stand-in. The same shape shows up in interest rates, in housing, in the time tax of unreliable transport. Scarcity forces short-horizon decisions, and short-horizon decisions are, in aggregate, more expensive.

  • A buffer turns emergencies into inconveniences.
  • Durable goods reward whoever can pay upfront.
  • Time saved compounds the same way money does.

Why it matters

Naming the pattern is useful because it moves the blame off the individual and onto the structure. You don't fix the boots theory with budgeting tips — you fix it by making the durable option reachable.
