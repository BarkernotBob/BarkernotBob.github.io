## **From:** Christy R Holliday

Tommy,

Change management is a critical part of any development process. The form was just a quick brain dump of the kinds of questions we should be asking before implementing system change requests and new features. It can be adjusted and would be utilized by analysts as well as your team. Our end users are accustomed to requesting features, and we are comfortable responding with customization because that was the perk of a home-grown system like AC2. Now, every change has larger implications because of considerations like moving to the Cloud and the dual nature of PE and PC (two separate systems that interact and affect one another). 

Since going live, we have received requests (often from just a single end user) to:

- Move a screen to a different part of the flow 
- Move a button
- Change a default value for a field
- Add Accordions
- Move a validatios
- And lots of others

These change requests need to be vetted in some way. There are also enhancements in the PE backlog that were captured as something we "probably need to do someday", which also need vetted. I don’t see any downside to asking good questions and having a process for evaluating these requests. The goal is not to make more work for anyone, but to ensure we have a way to objectively evaluate and respond to requests for the system to work differently than the original design.

## **From:** Eric T Ade

Hello everyone

The topic of change management for enhancement requests has come up several times in recent PC/PE Support Retros. While this process was actively followed during the PolicyCenter implementation, it has gradually faded over the past year as we transitioned into support mode.

Recently, a few of us met to discuss how to restart this process. We’ve reached a point where we have some ideas—and a few questions—and would like to review them with App Support to ensure the approach works for everyone.

First, we need to define the boundary between App Support’s role in gathering information from agents and underwriters (i.e., tracking) and the point at which a ticket should be created. The development/support team does not need to receive all feedback unless the number of requests surpasses a certain threshold.

This led us to some questions:

- How will App Support track these requests? What existing tools can help streamline the tracking of similar requests?
- What are the criteria to create a ticket for development work?
- number of requests - what is the threshold for creating a ticket (5,10, etc...)
- any other factors to tip the scale from monitoring/tracking to actively working on a development?
- Once a ticket is created, what is the process for determining if and when we will work on the request?
- Christy has drafted an initial version of a request form to help the team navigate the process. The form is attached to this email.

I’ll schedule time for us to discuss this. In the meantime, I wanted to send an intro email to get everyone thinking about the topic before the meeting. Thanks!
