# Body
**THE [THEOLOGOS] PROJECT**

For my children—Micah, Ezekiel, Hannah, Grace, Zechariah, and those unborn—someday, we will know Him face-to-face.

# **PREFACE**

I’ve long desired to keep a record of what I believe for my little pilgrims should I leave this world sooner than expected. Some find this thought morbid, or perhaps slightly paranoid. I suppose it is mildly driven by fear—however, not the kind of fear you might suspect. At 17 years in the Army—and all of those in Special Forces—I’ve become accustomed to dealing with fears. Three years before Micah was born, I was a much younger, stronger, and faster Green Beret than I am today. I found myself living in the middle of a small Afghan village, surrounded by Taliban day and night. Your beautiful mother had been uprooted, leaving her entire life in Florida to be with me in Tennessee, just so I could leave and live out childhood dreams of playing cowboys and Indians. Her patience and loyalty are only surpassed by those of her mother.

My childhood dreams became reality. I was in the Wild West, and for months on end, I was getting into about two gunfights a day. I was having a blast. Hearing rounds zip past your head while having grenades explode nearby instills a certain type of fear. But the kind of fear that has motivated me to start writing is a different type altogether. I wasn’t afraid to die back then. Your mother would have been crushed, but she would have moved on. That may sound cold to those outside of military life, but it’s true. A beautiful woman in her young twenties with nothing but a German Shepherd and a half million dollars from my life insurance policy would have been snatched up by the first gentlemen who were capable, after she was done mourning… At least, that’s how your selfish father saw it.

If you had asked me to describe myself before 2016, I would have told you, “I’m a Green Beret.” I wanted to be a Green Beret ever since the age of six, when I watched First Blood with my gun-toting grandmother from the Republic of Texas. But everything changed after 2016. That childhood cowboy in Afghanistan died—and I didn’t even care. I became disenchanted with the Army overnight. I was no longer a Green Beret; I was a father.

Being a father is much more stressful than getting shot at. Trying to keep your kids alive is much more difficult than keeping yourself alive. I could not have imagined the kind of love that I would have for you children until after you were given to me. But that wasn’t the only emotion that I experienced in new ways. You children forced me to face a new type of patience (an area I admittedly still suck at). I experienced a new kind of frustration the first time your mother left Micah with me alone—and he cried for four hours until she returned. But looking back, I can be proud that he never gave up. That boy had no quit in him. Obviously—a Green Beret’s son.

Returning to the topic at hand—a new type of fear—I get nervous flying civilian airliners now. Quite the difference from where I once was. This stems from the responsibilities I bear as your father. I could list many, but only one matters. I have been sent here by the King to lead you little pilgrims to His Celestial City, and there’s no one on this earth more qualified to accomplish this mission than your mother and me. That’s why God has entrusted you to us. I am not the best Green Beret. I was never the best in school. I wasn’t the best at sports. On a planet with 8 billion people, very few people are the best at anything. I’m no exception. But I am the best dad you’ll ever have on this earth. Because I’m the only one you’ve got. And that’s a terrifying thought, way more than anything I’ve ever experienced. I’ve been given a no-fail mission, and the stakes are high. They have eternal consequences. My work as a father is the most important mission I’ve ever been assigned, and it’s also the scariest.

However, we are no longer slaves to fear, my child. We are children of the King. We fear no man; we fear God. There are many things worse than death—living in fear, dying a slave, being separated from God. Now, in all these things, we are more than conquerors through Him, who loved us. For I am convinced that neither death nor life, nor angels nor rulers, nor things present nor things to come, nor powers, nor heights nor depths, nor anything else in all of creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord.

<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">People like me know how to plan and execute missions.</mark> Part of that process involves contingency planning to prepare for the likely outcomes of uncontrollable mission variables. This project is my contingency plan. I have many things I want to teach you. Most of the topics have been introduced to you already—in seed form—but you’re currently too young to understand how I see God’s Kingdom unfolding right before our eyes. This series is written for you, my children, to be read and built upon in your adulthood, long after I have departed from this world. It is not crafted for your young minds nor for the casual reader, but as a testament of my love, a roadmap for your pilgrimage, and a record of my deepest convictions about the King who sent me to guide you to His great city. Should you encounter these pages in my absence, I pray they stir both your intellect and your memory of the fatherly affection I held for you. Our family should make every effort to love the Lord our God with all our mind, heart, soul, and strength.

This work is intentionally rigorous, employing a vocabulary and conceptual framework that reflects the beautifully complex reality of the created order. Yet, perplexingly, the details that will follow are also surprisingly simple. You could say quite literally that my geometric understanding of this complex—yet simple—Kingdom is simplex in the truest sense of the word. Because of the simplexity present in our discussion, theological and academic precision is required to grapple with the infinite truth found in Jesus Christ. I have not simplified these ideas, for the mysteries of the Cosmos and the Logos demand a life of study and seeking, as I have pursued. The language—complex, precise, and unapologetically dense—is meant to challenge you, to invite you into the same disciplined exploration that shaped my understanding. If terms or concepts elude you at first, take heart; study them as I did, for this is part of the journey toward Him who is Truth. And besides, simplifying the concepts would do no good. As the series progresses, an understanding of the complexities involved will be necessary for further development of the topics we will discuss. The best way to approach this is to jump into the deep end, and over time, the terms and concepts will become more natural to you. You will eventually understand them through exposure and repetition.

The oscillation between scholarly discourse and direct address to you as “my little pilgrims” is deliberate—a nostalgic thread to remind you of our bond amidst the weight of these ideas. This is not a paper for the uninitiated or for “other readers”; it is yours, a legacy to be unpacked as you travel through life. As the [THEOLOGOS] project unfolds across future papers, the framework I present will grow more scientific and intricate, rendering early complexity not only necessary but foundational. Definitions and explanations are woven within the text or in the footnotes to aid your comprehension over time, supplemented by a glossary for reference. I may introduce a word or term before that term has been defined in depth. This is by design. These concepts take time to form in the mind before taking shape. If a word isn’t fully grasped, that’s okay. Keep reading and give the concept some time to form. Occasionally, I might give multiple explanations for a single topic. This is to aid in the process of comprehension and to allow the concept time to take shape in your mind.

Approach this not as a quick read but as a lifelong navigator’s guide for your journey to the Kingdom. Study, reflect, and return to the Axiom. Christ is building His kingdom—He is preparing a place for us—and I want to take you there. If the King wills it, you can follow me most of the way, but a day will come when a fork appears on our path, and we must part ways—for a little while. When that day comes, I’ll leave you these letters, encouraging you to cling to Christ and the Scriptures and to finish the race. Run to the King, my child! With or without me, you will find your way; the Lord will make your paths straight. Repent and believe in the Lord Jesus Christ, and you will be saved. He’s the author and finisher of our faith.

# **SECTION I: INTRODUCTION**

The [THEOLOGOS] project did not begin as an abstraction. It began in frustration—born from doctrinal disputes among well-meaning brothers. I found opponents standing on different epistemic foundations [^1] depending on the subject being discussed. This inconsistency troubled me, for it revealed a lack of unity in how we approach truth—a lesson I want you, my little pilgrims, to heed as you build your faith on the Solid Rock. All of those involved confessed Christ as Lord and the Scriptures as the only infallible rule of faith and practice. [^2] However, saying you’re standing on Scripture is merely a declaration of ‘what.’ It doesn’t explain or justify ‘how’ you’re employing those Scriptures or ‘why’ it matters. Understanding the underlying hermeneutic [^3] and epistemic methodologies is a matter of knowing the ‘how’ and ‘why’ of our approach to the Scriptures. This inconsistency prompted me to seek a unified method, leading to the question: How are we using Scripture to discern truth? What is our actual epistemic foundation? I want to teach you how to follow Christ—not merely that you should. Hypocrites can declare the ‘what’ of what the Scriptures say. Christians show you ‘how’ to live them and ‘why’ it matters.

**PROBLEM STATEMENT**

Many people use different lenses to defend their biblical positions. Sometimes, this involves selecting a preferred doctrine, a favorite systematic theology [^4], a respected theologian, or simply relying on denominational tradition as the foundational premise for their arguments. Admittedly, most would likely take issue with this charge. However, once their defense is dissected down to the irreducible primitives [^5] on which their argument stands, it’s very common to discover a different epistemic foundation depending on the topic under review. This is misguided and will lead to error, a pitfall I urge you, my children, to avoid. I must say, however, that I am not assigning malicious intent to anyone who’s ever been inconsistent. Many well-meaning Christians do not even realize they are standing on an improper foundation. They declare the right things; they may even begin with the right presuppositions. But they often err in their implementation—usually due to a lack of understanding about the foundational categories on which their positions rely. Put simply, they just haven’t thought about their beliefs deeply enough. I believe some of this stems from a general lack of exposure to the categories of classical western thought as classified in the Trivium and Quadrivium [^6]. I will have much to say about these subjects in later writings—however, that is not the focus of this paper.

Scripture points to only one hermeneutical lens for analysis and only one epistemic foundation upon which all knowledge must be understood: the Lord Jesus Christ. Admittedly, many people I speak with about doctrinal matters would agree with that statement. Yet their beliefs, when reduced to their axiomatic foundations [^7], are built on shifting elements—seemingly selected for their assumed persuasive power in winning an argument. It’s as if they have a buffet of validated premises at their disposal and they choose the one they believe will best make their current case. It is far less common to find a position that is intentionally structured from first principles [^8], purposely built to ensure the conclusion is linked through every premise and grounded in the axiom by an unbreakable chain of logic. I believe this is the root of most disagreements among those who share the same presuppositions and claim the same Christian worldview but find themselves on opposite ends of foundational doctrinal issues. In truth, the two sides do not share the same epistemic foundation.

It is my strongest conviction that Christ is preeminent over all things (and I mean all). This includes hermeneutical methodologies and every systematic approach to Scripture you can imagine. A proper Christology [^9] is the only lens and the only foundation by which we can understand anything rightly. Other doctrinal lenses and systematic approaches certainly reveal truth, some more than others. But they only do so according to the level of intensity in which they reflect Christ. Anything other than Him possesses no ability in itself to reveal anything. The more closely a selected interpretive lens reflects Christ, the more truth it will be able to discern. But it is a logical fallacy and a dangerous mistake to assume that because a particular methodology has yielded truth before, it can continue to do so universally—or even at all. Only Christ reveals truth universally. He is Revelation, He is Truth, and He alone is the One in whom all wisdom and knowledge are hidden. There are numerous hermeneutical lenses or systematic approaches people will offer for the sound interpretation of Scripture. Allow me to save you some time on your journey, my child—I’ve tried them all—there’s only one worth holding onto: Christology. And, if you cling to Christ as your sole interpretive tool, you will quickly find, He’s not only the key to unlocking the Scriptures—He’s the key to unlocking everything. He is Truth itself.

**THESIS STATEMENT**

My thesis for life is simple: The source, mechanism, and terminus of all being, knowing, and value can only be found in Jesus Christ. Everything that can be known exclusively comes through Him. Without Him, there is no life, no knowledge, and no meaning or purpose. Jesus Christ is the Self-Revealing, Self-Mediating, and Self-Interpreting God [^10]. That’s it. That’s the meaning of life. Jesus Christ is the meaning of life.

**PURPOSE STATEMENT**

My aim in this project is to offer a symbolic logic [^11] of the Christocentricity that permeates all things (Colossians 1:17) [^12]. I have no doubt this will be difficult to follow for those unaccustomed to spatial reasoning—and I admit I am not the most succinct explainer of things. I must also emphasize I do not claim that anyone must approach Scripture with my symbolic logic structure in mind. This is simply a glimpse into how I analyze truth claims, a method I share to guide you, my little pilgrims, in your pursuit of the Truth who is Christ (John 14:6). I’m not much of a verbal thinker, as you will soon realize. When I read the Scriptures, I set data into categories, place doctrines into clusters, and form those clusters into shapes, and then I take those shapes and build, seeing in my mind’s eye how all of God’s truths fit together. Some people write systematic theologies on paper—I’m constructing one in a three-dimensional thought-space [^13]. When I read the Scriptures, I rarely wrestle with ‘what’ they say. I spend most of my time studying ‘how’ they fit together as a cohesive unit. If I stumble across two paradoxical passages that seemingly contradict, it doesn’t take long for me to move past the ‘what’. Scripture is very clear. I know both paradoxical passages in question are true. It says what it says. I don’t get to determine what the Scriptures say, rather, Scripture gets to determine how I think about them (2 Timothy 3:16–17). So, how do these paradoxical verses fit together? The hardest doctrines to reconcile in the Scriptures will never be solved by wrestling with ‘what’ they say. God has spoken. Paradoxical passages are reconciled by understanding ‘how’ they harmonize with each other. And—if I may declare the end from the beginning—you will find the answer is always found in Jesus Christ (and I mean always).

Whether or not you grasp my visualization of the metalogic [^17] found in Christ is immaterial. What matters is the truth I aim to uphold: that Christ alone is the only proper foundation and lens by which we approach Scripture. In these last days, God has spoken through His Son (Hebrews 1:1–3). Many have arrived at this conclusion without employing any of the geometric methods I present here. I am not trying to convince you to adopt my way of thinking. In that regard, the symbolic details that follow are irrelevant. I present them only because I have no better way of explaining to you how I perceive the structural coherence Christ brings to the Cosmos [^18]. This is simply how I see His world. So, I encourage you to lay hold of the only True Axiom in any discussion in whatever manner suits your disposition. What matters most is that you arrive at that axiom. The Triune God has revealed Himself in Jesus Christ (John 1:18). He is the foundational source, mechanism, and terminus of all things—and the only appropriate hermeneutical lens and epistemic foundation. The Son of God is the sole axiom of existence.

**INTRODUCTORY SUMMARY**

This paper, [THEOLOGOS] I, presents a Christ-centered epistemic and hermeneutical framework—sometimes referred to as “the cube.” It is a representation of the deepest layers of reality and serves as an interactive model of how I mentally navigate these spaces. Centered on Jesus Christ as the sole axiom, the cube models the structural coherence of all being, knowing, and value through four irreducible triads: domains, axes, relations, and first principles. There are other triadic forms we will come across in future papers; however, the four mentioned here will be the focus of this paper.

•  The domains—Physical, Metaphysical, Spiritual—encompass all existence, coexisting as modal realities (Ephesians 1:10).

•  The axes—Epistemological, Axiological, Ontological—structure dimensions of thought, addressing “how,” “why,” and “what” of intelligibility (Colossians 2:3).

•  The relations—Dependence, Existence, Causality—bind and order creation’s logical coherence, reflecting Christ as “the way, the truth, and the life” (John 14:6).

•  The principles—Holy Spirit, Reason, Scripture—verify truth, harmonizing divine and human cognition (2 Timothy 3:16–17), as Irenaeus and Athanasius affirm in Against Heresies and On the Incarnation.

All these foundational triads flow from a single point of origin labeled “Christ”; the cube unfolds as an ontological grammar, validated through a recursive scientific method constrained by Christo-logical coherence. It bears the Trinitarian imprint. Creation displays God’s divine nature—His essence, attributes, and relations—as articulated in Romans 1 and upheld in Nicene and Chalcedonian orthodoxy. This framework is meant to assist you in assessing truth claims by conforming everything to the Logos who holds all things together (Colossians 1:17). While it may seem daunting at first, its symbolic representations and metalogic are meant to illuminate the Christologic connections that are found in all things and encourage a lifelong desire for conformity to the image of the Son.

I’m not inventing anything new. The fundamental elements that will emerge have long been recognized as foundational categories in metaphysics. However, instead of starting with these categories and developing a theology, we are beginning with Christ and developing a metaphysic [^19]. In the classical Western world, Ontology [^20], Epistemology [^21], and Axiology [^22] were identified as taxonomic archetypes spanning all philosophical discourse and thought. These form one of many irreducible triads that will emerge in my framework. This paper will see the Christological emergence of domains, relations, and principles, which constitute primitive components in our epistemic foundation. The domains—Physical, Metaphysical, and Spiritual—define the realms of existence (Ephesians 1:10) [^23]. The axes—Epistemological, Axiological, and Ontological—structure the dimensions of thought (Colossians 2:3) [^24]. The relations—Dependence [^25], Existence [^26], and Causality [^27]—bind and order creation’s logical coherence. The first principles of truth—the Holy Spirit, Reason, and Scripture—guide the individual in truth’s discovery, verification, and validation (2 Timothy 3:16–17) [^28]. In the ancient Western world, the Logos [^29] was identified as the unmoved mover [^30] and the first principle of all rationality in the Cosmos—centuries before the first coming of our Lord and Savior. The first principles that will emerge in our structure are a triadic reflection of that Logos [^31]. The same is true of the distinctions between the physical and metaphysical realms. Philosophers have debated endlessly about the irreducibly complex paradoxes that lie at the foundations of metaphysical thought. These debates continue to this day. But Christ harmonizes all of these things in His very being (Colossians 1:17) [^32] [^33].

This cube is not a metaphor. It is a structured ontological grammar [^34]—first realized through the harmonization of doctrine with the centrality of Christ [^35]. Scripture doesn’t name these classical metaphysical categories. There isn’t a chapter in the Bible that lays out a taxonomy of philosophy. Still, it would be a grave mistake to think the Bible doesn’t address metaphysics. On the contrary, the Scriptures loudly proclaim the answer to every problem metaphysics has ever raised (Colossians 2:3). The solution comes only by subjecting everything in existence to the Preeminent One so that you may see all things through Him who is Truth (John 14:6). When the Logos took on flesh and dwelt among us, being the ultimate Revelation of God to men, we were given the solution to every problem (John 1:14) [^36]. In Christ, we discover that every fundamentally complex problem finds its answer in His very Being [^37]. The most concerning problem of all: “How can I, a sinner, stand blameless before a Holy God? What must I do to be saved?” The solution, just like every other solution, is found in the person of Jesus Christ. The elements of my epistemology that we will discuss are not arbitrary divisions, nor are they functions of human imagination. They are irreducible primitives—fractal emanations of the true eternal order—the Cosmic Kingdom: multimodal, metaphysical, hyper-dimensional, metalogical, and inter-relational. Simply put, they’re Christocentric realities.

**A WORD OF CAUTION**

Christology must be at the center of every doctrine. Christ is at the center of everything else. Scripture, creation, covenant, kingdom—they all must find their proper “place” in relation to Him. So, we will set out to find their place in Him. As we plot these doctrines, we will discover that the structure of logic itself will emerge, revealing a deeper, dynamic cohesion—this is what it means to be metalogical. In my next paper, [THEOLOGOS] II, we will discover that logic itself takes a Christological form. My little pilgrims, my aim is for you to see all things through Christ—He is all in all. In the upcoming sections, I will detail how my Christological journey of discovery began by plotting biblical doctrines in geometric thought space—a truly Christian Metaphysic.

But didn’t I just say this project is about Christ—what’s this metaphysics talk about? Metaphysics is not a framework to be applied to Christ—may it never be! It does not reveal Him. It cannot. Metaphysics reveals the logical structures embedded by God into the invisible fabric of creation. It displays His handiwork as part of the created order. All of creation points to the triune God through the works of His Son—both visible and invisible—and the metaphysical world is no exception. Knowing Jesus Christ is the only way to rightly understand anything. We don’t apply metaphysics to Christ. We apply Christ to metaphysics. You don’t need to read Aristotle to understand the Trinity. You need a higher Christology [^60]. Aristotle didn’t invent metaphysical categories—he didn’t even discover them. Aristotle is known as the father of metaphysics because he was the first to systematize these concepts in writing. God created the metaphysical world, and His fingerprints are deeply ingrained on every foundational principle that Aristotle ever wrote about. And guess who never found Christ? Aristotle. He never found Christ because you cannot ascend to Truth through metaphysics. Truth must descend and reveal itself to you—and Christ did. You don’t need Aristotle to have a deeper understanding of God’s essence. If you want a deeper understanding of God in Himself, you need a deeper understanding of Christ. He is the final and ultimate Revelation of God, and the fullness of the Godhead dwells bodily in Him (Colossians 2:9) [^61]. Any other epistemic lens suggested for intellectual or spiritual ascension to the deep truths of the Godhead is wholly inadequate and will lead to error. If Christ isn’t the door you enter on your way to the King, you’re walking in darkness. Who is it that can ascend? Only He who descended (Ephesians 4:9–10) [^62].

I am delivering this warning at the start of our journey because I recognize that my own thinking is very metaphysically oriented. And there’s nothing wrong with that, for that is how God made me. However, you must understand that I am not encouraging you to look to metaphysics. I want you to look to Christ. This section serves as a warning not to be carried away by the empty philosophies of this world (Colossians 2:8) [^63]. This isn’t a condemnation of metaphysics. It’s a condemnation of Christ-less metaphysics. I’ll restate my thesis for life as a reminder: The source, mechanism, and terminus of all being, knowing, and value can only be found in Jesus Christ. Everything that can be known exclusively comes through Him. Without Him, there is no life, no knowledge, and no meaning or purpose. Jesus Christ is the Self-Revealing, Self-Mediating, and Self-Interpreting God. As such, there is only one proper hermeneutical lens [^64] and only one epistemic foundation [^65] upon which all knowledge must be understood: the Lord Jesus Christ.

I am not discouraging you from obtaining knowledge. Quite the opposite. I encourage you to read everything, even Aristotle. Do not be afraid of knowledge. Knowledge is a weapon, but you must wield that weapon wisely. I read Aristotle the same way I read everything else—through a Christological lens. Christians read Christ into Aristotle; they don’t read Aristotle into Christ. The latter makes you an Aristotelian—and we are no such thing. All knowledge in isolation, no matter how profound, is meaningless information. Information is nothing more than a set of fragmented bytes of data that ultimately find their source and terminus in Jesus Christ. Do not suppose that knowledge and knowing are synonymous. I only know one thing: Jesus Christ is the Self-Revealing, Self-Mediating, and Self-Interpreting God [^50]. If you could trace every fact, concept, abstraction, doctrinal position, theological discovery, and sound argument through its logical chain of dependence, not stopping until you arrived at its source, you would find yourself staring at Jesus Christ—and I mean that as literally as possible. Every fact, concept, abstraction, doctrinal position, theological discovery, and sound argument is logically dependent upon something prior. If you follow that chain of dependence to its terminus, you’ll be left with the only true irreducible axiom that ever existed. When Christ said, “I am the Truth” (John 14:6) [^66], this wasn’t just a theological claim about His message. It was an ontological declaration [^67] about His very Being. It was necessary to separate the light from the darkness before beginning our journey. Now, we embark on a deep dive into the Christ-centered realm of metaphysics. As we explore, we will uncover truths along the way. Yet after all our searching, digging, and descending to the foundational epistemic bedrock, we will discover what Scripture has already stated plainly: only one truth stands immovably firm—Jesus Christ. I only know one thing.

# **SECTION II: THE POINT OF ORIGIN**

This is the story of how “the cube” was born—not from theory, but from pattern recognition. I previously noted my disapproval of selecting from a variety of hermeneutical lenses when defending doctrinal positions. In contrast, I believe one should consistently use the same framework, regardless of the doctrine in question. Our key ring for opening Truth’s gate should only hold one key. I make a theological plea for consistency, a metaphysical demand for a solid foundation. And that foundation, by every law of faith and reason, must be Jesus Christ and no other. Not Christ as one category among others, but Christ as the foundation, the structural framework, and the teleological aim [^38] of all doctrine—the Logos [^39] in whom all being, knowing, and valuing are bound (Colossians 1:17).

Come, my little pilgrims, let us take a journey through the genesis of my epistemic voyage. I set out with one destination in mind: to find a way to make clear the centrality of Christ. Admittedly, it turned into something beautifully complex, possibly too complicated for most to follow. Nevertheless, I continued pressing forward because I became fascinated with the connections that began forming before my eyes. I found connections I had not even been looking for. I would notice a pattern, set out to resolve it, and after making the connection, a new pattern would appear. This became a recurring theme that continues to this day. The truth revealed in nature—even metaphysical space—is never ending, because the God who created it is infinite. There is no bottom to the depths of His Wisdom (Romans 11:33) [^40]. I do not expect you to think like me, but I expect you to arrive at the same conclusion. We all have our own odyssey to embark on. The Lord makes our paths straight (Proverbs 3:6). I will introduce the epistemic elements in the same order I stumbled upon them, for even in that, a pattern emerges—a lesson to be learned.

**A LITTLE POINT CALLED CHRIST**

At first, I envisioned only a rough space—you might say it was formless and void (Genesis 1:2) [^41]. In the center of a blank sheet of paper, I placed a dot labeled “Christ.” I wrote “Creation” on the left side of the paper and “Scripture” on the right. I then connected them with a line. The segment left of Christ brought to mind natural theology [^42], and to the right, special revelation [^43]. Christ stepped into the natural world He created, and Christ is the Living Word of God (John 1:14). It seemed fitting to center these doctrines around Him. The next doctrines I placed were the doctrine of the Covenants and the doctrine of the Kingdom. Christ fulfilled the covenants, and He reigns over the kingdom. I did not want to cause confusion by adding redemptive doctrines to a line that only made space for doctrines of revelation. The revelatory doctrines on the X-axis teach us “how” God reveals, but the Kingdom and Covenants teach us “what” God reveals. So, I drew a second line intersecting Christ, and our odyssey had begun. I now had a Y-axis to place doctrines relating to redemption. I soon needed a third line for the doctrine of God and the doctrine of Man. These doctrines answered “who” is involved in revelation. I couldn’t place them on the two lines I currently had without causing confusion. Our arrangement must be a logically coherent representation [^44] of biblical teachings. So, I drew a third line intersecting with Christ, and… Eureka! I have found it[^44a].

**EUREKA!**

I didn’t know it at the time—but—this was only the first of many discoveries I would stumble upon. Do you have any idea what you have just witnessed? I certainly didn’t at first. But at present, knowing how far this road will take us, I find the events in this section astonishing. In the beginning, there was a blank sheet of nothing and a single constraint: logical coherence. After the preeminent point, logic demanded the emergence of a line. A second line became necessary, and a plane was formed. Soon, a third line was needed to maintain internal coherence. We have just created Euclidean space—a Cartesian cube [^45]. You have just witnessed the unfolding of a three-dimensional metaphysical space from a single point. The only axiom: Christ. The only constraint: Logic. It was Christo-logical [^46] … In the beginning was the Logos (John 1:1).

This is the true story of the Logos creating the Cosmos [^47] and beginning His descent into creation, embarking on the hero’s journey culminating with Christ enthroned in eternity reigning over the Cosmic Kingdom—and it’s embedded in the fabric of reality. And as unbelievable as that may sound, I promise you, if we break down the elements of existence to their irreducible and primitive components, Christ’s signature is imprinted on every Planck frame[^47a] of the universe. We’re going exploring. I don’t expect you to see it yet, my child—but I pray every day that you will. Everyone who finishes their journey and arrives at that Celestial City will see. So let’s keep walking, pilgrim. You’re not the hero of this story. It’s His odyssey. We’re on a pilgrimage. And God said, let there be light (Genesis 1:3) [^48] … The true light, which gives light to everyone, was coming into the world (John 1:9) [^49].

I plotted the covenants at one extreme of the y-axis and the kingdom at the other, because Christ is the Lamb slain before the foundation of the world [^51] and the King enthroned at the end of the age [^52]. The more I plotted, the more harmony I saw. The point turned into a line. The line turned into a plane. The plane turned into a cube. Patterns began to emerge. I need to give you a solid rock to stand on, so that everything else I write will be interpreted in the correct manner. I believe it is my highest calling—as your father—to lead you to Him. I do not want anything else.

I know you won’t understand most of this for many years. And that’s alright. You’re still too young to care about diagrams or footnotes. But none of us are promised tomorrow. As I write this, I once again find myself playing cowboys and Indians—and I am painfully aware of how fleeting life can be. We are but grass in the field—here today, gone tomorrow. My only purpose in life is to lead you to Christ. Children, I can see Him so clearly now. His fingerprints are everywhere. But it wasn’t always this way. I remember what it felt like to doubt. I remember being shaken by facts that seemed to disprove God and having no answers. I remember wondering if I was truly saved. I remember reading Scripture and feeling nothing. I remember what it was like to be lost—even while sitting in church.

That’s why this paper is so dense—and at least ten years ahead of your ability to comprehend. I do not want to simply explain what I believe. I want to give you a grammar for your journey. I am mapping out my spatial reasoning in the most literal sense possible. I’m showing you, in Euclidean space, how I think about Christ. The more I write with this Christological structure in mind, the more clearly you’ll see how I think—not just what I think. And in that way, I’ll keep teaching you long after I’m gone. You won’t need to ask, “What would Dad think about this?” You’ll already know how I would have reasoned it—through the Axiom, through the grammar of the Logos, through the inspired Scriptures. That is what I desire to leave you: a living Christological grammar—a metalogical blueprint of the Cosmic Kingdom we call home.

But understand this—what we’ve explored so far is only the beginning. Every connection we’ve uncovered has emerged solely through the analysis of Third Domain data—and even that from only a small set of doctrinal coordinates. We have arrived here simply by conforming doctrinal categories to Christ. But we still have two more domains to walk through before the epistemic foundation is completely laid. And the same coherence, the same radiant truths waiting beneath the surface that we saw here, are waiting to be uncovered in the other domains as well. I can already see the patterns emerging. One day, you will too.

**[THEOLOGOS]**

I have intentionally withheld my reasoning for naming this project [THEOLOGOS]—until now. If, by some chance, the rationale has eluded you, allow me to conclude with it plainly. I began this paper by saying that I have been sent here by the King—to show you, my children, the way to His city. Therefore, the greatest gift I could ever give you is a proper theology, grounded in the Scriptures. The only way to know the King is to read and believe His message.

The Greek word for Theology is Theologia, a compound of Theos (God) and Logos (discourse). [^228] So, Theologia simply means “discourse about God.” But I did not write this paper merely to discourse about Him—I want to introduce you to Him. And that is only possible by knowing the Lord Jesus Christ through the ministry of the Holy Spirit. The logos in [THEOLOGOS] is not just a discourse. It is the Logos of John 1 who came into the world and dwelt among us—the Word who became flesh. It is the Logos who is the exact imprint of God’s essence. It is the Logos in whom the fullness of the Godhead dwells bodily. This Logos is Theos. Many church fathers noted the significance of the pairing which formed Theologia—and still, I desire to bring it to light once more.

In the very structure of the word [THEOLOGOS], scriptural truths begin to unfold. In [THEOLOGOS], you can see the Son begotten by the Father. You can see that the Son and the Father are one. You can perceive the distinction of Persons yet recognize the unity of essence. In [THEOLOGOS], you see the exclusivity of Christ: if you do not know the Son, you cannot know the Father—the Son and the Father are one. And if you have seen the Son, then you have seen the Father. In [THEOLOGOS], the Father is never revealed apart from the Son—He is always revealed through the Son. In THEOS, you see the eternal Source. In LOGOS, you behold the Axiom through whom the Source is known. [THEOLOGOS] is not only the name of this project—it is the identity our epistemic foundation, our metalogical center, our Christological grammar. [THEOLOGOS] is our Axiomatic Source.

And finally, maybe you’re wondering why I have placed brackets around [THEOLOGOS]. Remember when I said that it is impossible to find God within creation because you can’t place God a box? Well, that’s still true. We didn’t find God. God stepped into creation and revealed Himself to us—and He didn’t show up in a box. He arrived in His Temple. Jesus Christ is the only instance of existence in which we can say, “the fullness of the Godhead dwells bodily” in Him. Jesus Christ is the only instance of existence in which can say “He is the exact imprint of God’s essence.” Jesus Christ is the Self-Revealing, Self-Mediating, and Self-Interpreting God. He is God in the flesh.

No one has ever seen God; the only begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father, he has made him known. (John 1:18)
