This [[Men at Arms, Samuel Vimes, "The reason that the rich were so..."|Boots Theory Quote]] sums up the view. 

[Wikipedia entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boots_theory)

Essentially, the less you have, the more you're forced to buy low price, which leads to more spending because these things wear out and break faster, which leads to more long term spending.

https://images.app.goo.gl/8p6i6ncuzRDMXEwD6

Article that pushes back specifically on the idea that spending less is why the rich are rich. 

https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/67tR3ZesBL3zfy8sA/against-boots-theory

This reminds me that I'd like to look into [[Correlation does not equal causation|Single Factor Analysis]], as part of the error here seems to be that one factor is being both oversimplified and raised up as THE reason poor/rich are where they are. This looks to be related to [[Confounding]] ([Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confounding),) and why [[Correlation does not equal causation]] ([Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation_does_not_imply_causation).)
