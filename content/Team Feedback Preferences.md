# Spencer
FEEDBACK 

Do you like it to be direct and to the point, no sugar coating it or "waiting for the right time"?

I do like direct feedback. I don't like it being fluffed up, it makes me feel like it isn't truthful. I prefer to be given feedback that is helpful in my growth and that often doesn't require any fluff. 

Do you like a heads up, so you can be mentally prepared?

I do prefer a heads up to mentally prepared as feedback can sometime make me double guess myself. It gives me time to prepare and gather my footing before I receive helpful and growth minded feedback. 

Do you prefer to hear it in the moment, or after some time has passed? 

I prefer it to be mindful of the situation, but I don't enjoy when time has passed and it seems like a late thought. 

Do you like it written out or prefer it discussed? 

I don't have a preference, but I usually find myself having questions if it is written out, so please be prepared for that.

# Christy
I prefer direct communication but do appreciate a heads up that it is coming. I agree with you that a compliment sandwich isn't necessary and feels disingenuous. I think it's always better to wait for some time to pass so that emotion can play less of a factor in both the giving and receiving of the feedback. 

In person is usually best, but I do also like having things in writing. If it were a really contentious situation, I think following up in writing is a good way to ensure understanding and allow for processing time. Sometimes what we "hear" can be swayed by reactivity, so having the clarity of a written response is helpful. Again, only really necessary for especially difficult conversations. 

What I do think is key is that feedback is a learning process for both people and allows for back and forth communication. It shouldn't be a one way street of "here is my suggestion for how you should have done something differently". Real world example: I might say to Tommy that I was offended by his response when I demoed system changes and he called it "GROSS" and asked for a bunch of changes. Ideally, Tommy would acknowledge that his words were not professional and could have been better chosen. Maybe Tommy would want to explain that because we have worked together so closely in the past, he figured he could talk more freely with me and I would understand. And I would acknowledge that under different circumstances that might be the case, but things between our groups have been tense so there isn't the same playful vibe. Ideally, we are both listening to each other and gaining context and understanding while also seeking to adjust our behavior based on the feedback. There might not be this much back and forth with a manager/employee conversation, but I think allowing for a response to the feedback is still a good practice.
