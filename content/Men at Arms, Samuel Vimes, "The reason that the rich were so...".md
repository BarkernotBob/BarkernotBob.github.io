>  The reason that the rich were so rich, Vimes reasoned, was because they managed to spend less money. Take boots, for example. ... A really good pair of leather boots cost fifty dollars. But an affordable pair of boots, which were sort of OK for a season or two and then leaked like hell when the cardboard gave out, cost about ten dollars. ... But the thing was that good boots lasted for years and years. A man who could afford fifty dollars had a pair of boots that'd still be keeping his feet dry in ten years' time, while a poor man who could only afford cheap boots would have spent a hundred dollars on boots in the same time **and would still have wet feet.**

# Thoughts

This is certainly a constraint that must be acknowledged. 
Long term spending can be increased by consistent low spending. 

Think also of things that correlate to the **Wet Feet**, such as: 

Spending less on food can decrease nutrition. This could lowers life expectancy
Decrease cognitive function
Leading to lower earnings
Lower quality of life

Not having money for something early (a house, car, school) can lead to foregoing those, or paying for them via loan. 

Quote gets a lower rating, though, because to call this the reason why people are rich is certainly wrong. See linked article on [[Boots Theory]]
