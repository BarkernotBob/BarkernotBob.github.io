# Intro
When we're talking about money, we usually associate the word investment with a place we put our money where, over a certain amount of time, that money grows. This could be as safe as high yield savings account or as risky as bitcoin, but the premise of putting a little money in and hoping to get a larger amount out is the same. But an investment is not limited to something that grows more money, it can be anything we put our money into that we believe will yield value over time. Buying an asset such as a car, house, or boat, or giving money to a friend, your church, or a charity would all be investments, just in a different sense. 

To illustrate this point, let's just consider for a moment why anyone chooses to buy a brand new car. It is widely recognized that a new car becomes a used car when?: The moment you drive it off the dealership lot. And this now used car could be sold just a day later and you'll probably sell it for 15-20% less than what you bought it for. So if the one and only factor we consider is money, this is a BAD investment. So why does anyone do it? Because the car is an investment. Not the kind of investment where the resale value grows, but one where the added features, safety, reliability, luxury, status, or whatever other combination of factors are considered to be worth the additional monetary cost of the vehicle.
